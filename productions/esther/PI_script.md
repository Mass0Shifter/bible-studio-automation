# The Book of Esther — Prophetic Implication (script draft 1)

While the Book of Esther is often told as a royal love story, an orphan girl who wins the king's heart and becomes queen, it is actually one of the most suspenseful rescue stories in all of Scripture. And it is the only book in the Bible where the name of God never appears, yet His hand is on every page.

When it comes to the grand story of salvation, Esther operates as a hidden hand, a royal mediator, and a great reversal.

Here is the breakdown of Esther's significance to redemptive history.

## 1. The Hidden Hand (The God Who Is Never Named)

**The Silence:** No prayer is quoted, and no miracle is shown. Instead we find a sleepless king, a forgotten record, and a petition made at exactly the right moment.

**The Providence:** Mordecai trusts that deliverance will come, and he asks Esther, "who knoweth whether thou art come to the kingdom for such a time as this?" (Esther 4:14)

**The Salvation Link:** The God who seemed silent in Persia was quietly guarding the people through whom the Messiah would come. As the apostle Paul writes, "all things work together for good to them that love God." (Romans 8:28)

## 2. The Ancient Enemy (The War Against the Promised Seed)

**The Agagite:** Haman is called "the Agagite", widely understood as a descendant of Agag, king of Amalek, Israel's oldest enemy.

**The Decree:** He plots to destroy every Jew in the empire in a single day. Had he succeeded, the line of promise would have been cut off.

**The Salvation Link:** This is the long war first announced in Eden: "I will put enmity between thee and the woman, and between thy seed and her seed." (Genesis 3:15) Esther shows that no decree of man can stop the Seed God has promised. In the fulness of time, Christ was born from that very people.

## 3. The Royal Mediator (A Picture of Christ)

**The Risk:** To approach the king uncalled meant death. Esther fasts three days, then goes: "and if I perish, I perish." (Esther 4:16)

**The Sceptre:** "The king held out to Esther the golden sceptre that was in his hand. So Esther drew near, and touched the top of the sceptre." (Esther 5:2)

**The True and Better Esther:** Esther risked her life for her people; Jesus gave His. Esther was received by grace, and through Christ we are invited: "Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need." (Hebrews 4:16)

## 4. The Great Reversal (From Sorrow to Joy)

**The Gallows:** "So they hanged Haman on the gallows that he had prepared for Mordecai." (Esther 7:10)

**The Unchangeable Law:** The king's first decree could not be undone, so a second was written and sealed with the king's ring, a writing that "may no man reverse." (Esther 8:8)

**The Gospel Turn:** The day meant for destruction became the month "turned unto them from sorrow to joy." (Esther 9:22) At the cross, the very weapon the enemy raised against the Son became the enemy's own defeat. Christ "made a shew of them openly, triumphing over them in it." (Colossians 2:15) The sentence against us was real, but God wrote a better word, sealed in the blood of His Son.

## Summary

Esther reminds us that even when God seems silent, He is never absent. He raised up a queen for such a time as this, guarded the line of the promised Seed, and turned a day of death into a feast of joy. And in Jesus, the true and better Mediator, the King's sceptre is held out to all who will come. The hidden hand of God in Esther points us to the open, nail-scarred hands of Christ.
